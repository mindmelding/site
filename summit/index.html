How it works

↓
  1. 01

    Plan

    Pick how much climb you want, from 100 to 3,000 feet. Summit sends out a scout loop to learn how hilly your area is, then fans out searches until it has three walks that fit.

  2. 02

    Walk

    The plan is drawn in pencil and your walk inks over it. The barometer measures the climb and GPS keeps it honest, so the number you see is the number you earned.

  3. 03

    Bank it

    Every walk's gain goes toward your peak. The Goal page shows how high you are, the next landmark on the real route up, and when you'll summit at this pace.

Your peak, on a page

Pick Aconcagua and your walks start at the bottom of it. Pass 62% and you're at Plaza de Mulas base camp. Keep going and the page gets stamped Summited, then it moves to your summit shelf.

What it won't do

↓

It won't send your walks anywhere

Your walks, your peak and your history live on your phone. There's no account to make and no server holding your routes.

Planning shares two points and nothing else

To find routes, Summit sends your start and end points to the route service. The walk itself tracks offline, even in airplane mode.

It won't pad the number

Raw elevation data inflates climbs by a fifth or more. Summit only counts a climb once you've risen a few meters, on the plan and on the walk, so the wobble in the readings never adds up.

It won't nag you back on route

No rerouting and no alarms. If you leave the plan, the screen says so once and keeps counting.
